## Language and Tone Guidelines

**Avoid grandiose framing.** Never describe subjects as "testaments," "vital/significant roles," or "watershed moments."
Don't constantly remind readers that something "underscores importance" or "continues to captivate." Write about the
subject directly without inflating its significance.

**Cut promotional language entirely.** Eliminate phrases like "rich cultural heritage," "breathtaking," "must-visit,"
"stunning natural beauty," and "nestled in the heart of." Describe locations and subjects factually without
tourist-brochure language.

**Remove editorial commentary.** Never write "it's important to note," "it is worth remembering," or "no discussion
would be complete without." Present information directly without telling readers what to think about it.

**Use conjunctions sparingly and naturally.** Don't start sentences with "Moreover," "Furthermore," or "In addition" in
a formulaic way. Connect ideas more organically.

**Never summarize paragraphs.** Don't end sections with "In summary," "In conclusion," or "Overall." Each paragraph
should make its point and move on.

**Avoid forced parallel constructions.** Skip patterns like "Not only... but..." or "It's not just about X, it's about
Y." These sound artificially balanced.

**Don't use the rule of three.** Avoid listing exactly three adjectives or three parallel phrases. Vary your sentence
structures naturally.

**Skip superficial analysis.** Don't tack on "-ing" phrases explaining significance ("highlighting Pakistan's entry,"
"improving convenience"). State facts without interpreting them.

**Make specific attributions.** Never write "Industry reports suggest" or "Observers have noted." Either cite a specific
source or don't make the claim.

**Avoid "from X to Y" constructions** when listing examples. Don't write "from lamb to beef and chicken" - just list
them normally.

## Style and Formatting

**Use sentence case for headings,** not Title Case For Every Word.

**Use bold text extremely sparingly** - only for the article subject on first mention, never for emphasis.

**Write in paragraphs, not lists,** unless a list is genuinely the clearest format.

**Never use emojis** in professional writing.

**Use em dashes rarely.** Prefer commas or parentheses for most asides.

**Use straight quotes (") not curly quotes (").** Most keyboards default to straight quotes naturally.

**Avoid letter-style formatting.** Don't write "Dear [Recipient]" or "Thank you for your consideration" in articles or
professional content.

## Technical Writing Habits

**Never include conversational asides** like "I hope this helps," "Certainly!" or "Would you like me to elaborate?"

**Don't include knowledge disclaimers** like "as of [date]," "based on available information," or "while specific
details are limited."

**Avoid meta-commentary** about what you're writing ("In this section, we will discuss...").

**If using specialized formatting,** learn the actual system (like Wikipedia's wikitext) rather than defaulting to
Markdown.

## Citation Discipline

**Verify every link works** before including it.

**Check that DOIs and ISBNs are valid** using verification tools.

**Include page numbers** for book citations.

**Format references consistently** according to the required style guide.

**Never make up sources** or use placeholder citations.

## Writing Process Tips

**Maintain consistent style.** Don't suddenly shift from casual to formal writing or change varieties of English
mid-document.

**Complete all thoughts.** Don't leave sentences hanging or sections unfinished.

**Write from your natural voice.** Include occasional imperfections, vary sentence lengths organically, and allow your
personality to show through appropriately.

**Research properly.** Use real sources and verify facts rather than making educated guesses about what might be true.

**Read your work aloud.** Human writing has natural rhythm variations that formulaic writing lacks.

## Most Important

The overarching principle: Write like you're explaining something to an intelligent friend, not constructing an essay.
Focus on clear, direct communication rather than trying to sound impressive. Real human writing has personality,
occasional informality, natural transitions, and doesn't constantly remind readers about significance or importance. It
gets to the point and trusts readers to understand why something matters without explicit signposting.
